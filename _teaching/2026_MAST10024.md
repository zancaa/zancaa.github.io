This subject is a first-year undergraduate core elective for the Bachelor of Biomedicine (and also available as breadth for other degrees). In Semester 1, 2026, there were approximately 100 enrolments. The course covers the mathematics behind mass action kinetics, enzyme kinetics, hypothesis testing, linear regression and epidemic modelling. 

My contributions: 
- Redesigned weekly quizzes to make them more conceptual (questions matching pictures with text descriptions, for example), with feedback for every combination of choices a student could select
- Created new tutorial questions
- Created new revision materials
- Reordered material to be better aligned, and
- Adding a 'Part A' to every assignment, asking students to reflect on course material (sample questions include, 'a generative artifical intelligence tool produced the following answer to a previous assignment. Critique this response - what do you like or not like about it? How many marks do you think it would receive?'; 'take a photo of some work you did in class that you are proud of and why you are proud of it'; 'we learned the Principle of Superposition in class, spend 5 minutes searching for another method of solving first-order linear ordinary differential equations and compare it to the Principle of Superposition')

Outcomes:
- Student feedback suggests that students were happy with the amount of feedback they received in the subject
- Students enjoyed the tutorial material (some of which was existing)
- Students found it difficult to learn R

To do:
- Lecture content needs tweaking! (My opinion: less is more. Remove Hill kinetics and go into more depth, and more clearly, into ordinary differential equations, close this topic with Michaelis-Menten kinetics. Statistical concepts start too basic (e.g. calculating the mean of a sample), then get confusing quickly (particularly with some notational choices). The final topic should tie the modelling and statistics together, but currently does not do so meaningfully.)

Commentary on AI usage:
- I attempted to produce some additional exercises using generative AI (particularly, the University of Melbourne's in-house system, SparkAI, with existing materials provided). This did not produce any meaningful questions.
- Students anecdotally told me they tried to use generative AI to try to help them study and were not able to do so successfully.
- I think the above speaks to the slightly 'niche' approach in this subject (not as standard as some other subjects).
